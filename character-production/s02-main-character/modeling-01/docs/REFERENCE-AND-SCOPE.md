# Modeling review — approved reference and scope

Visual authority: the Season 02 character sheet supplied twice in this conversation. The image was visually inspected in chat; its binary file is not present in the attached filesystem, so this package does not claim a pixel-registered comparison or a source-image checksum.

Identity authority: `life-as-graphic-designer-ep01/src/characters/PixelCharacter.tsx`, `CharacterExpressions.ts` and `src/data/colors.ts`, read from the preserved recovery source. No edits made to those files.

## Reference observations used for modeling

- Black/brown-black layered hair; broad crown, stepped outer silhouette, asymmetrical swept front and low side locks. Large planes rather than a uniform Minecraft cap or a mass of tiny noisy cubes.
- Broad rectangular head, clipped chin corners; tall rectangular eye whites and dark pupils, thick brows, small cheek accents and minimal mouth.
- Cream pullover hoodie with layered hood at the neck, pale drawstrings with lime ends, wide cuffs, front kangaroo pocket and restrained hem.
- Tapered charcoal trousers and thick olive shoes with a flat sole and angular toe. No added logos or unrelated accessories.
- Voxel/chibi proportions and matte surfaces; narrow one-facet bevels provide light-catching edges without smoothing the silhouette into clay.

The design sheet's written 3.5–4-head note and its illustrated turnaround do not provide a calibrated orthographic scale. The first model is judged by the illustrated head-to-body silhouette, rather than claiming a precise match to that note.

## Authorized stage

User authorized Blender for modeling, materials and later rigging, with a mandatory user review of shape first. This deliverable stops at static character evaluation. No armature, skin weights, expression rig, animation, production GLB/GLTF or Episode 01 character integration is included.

Modeling components overlap at cloth joints to close the visible silhouette. This is not a claim of deformation-ready topology. Connected deformation, finger posing, facial expressions and joint tests belong to the later approved rigging stage.
